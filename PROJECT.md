# Why is it a problem?
Human populations tend to settle near water bodies such as rivers due to a variety of reasons, but usually due to ease of access to water, transport, economic activities among others. During heavy rains, they tend to burst their banks and flood waters encroach the nearby settlements. This results in property damage, loss of life, disease outbreak among others.\
However, rivers follow the historical topography of the land,pathways that rarely change over decades. During heavy rains, rivers burst their banks and floodwaters instinctively reclaim these natural paths, encroaching on nearby settlements. Furthermore, human activities such as agriculture reduce natural vegetation and loosen the soil, significantly increasing the velocity and risk of flash floods, leading to property damage, loss of life, and disease outbreaks.

# Who experiences it
People who settle near and/or along river banks, at the mouth of a river and along the river's path.

# Why does it matter now?
Following adverse weather patterns in climate change, extreme droughts lead to drying up of rivers for extensive periods of time, while heavy rains lead to flash floods along the dry riverbeds and river banks which catch settlements unawares. This results in extensive damages and losses.

# What are we trying to change
By mapping out the historical and topographical routes of rivers(both actively flowing and currently dried up)we can accurately predict the path of the water. This allows us to alert populations at risk before heavy rains hit. In the case of flash floods, settlements living downstream or along the predicted path will receive the hyper-local early warnings they need to evacuate in time.\
Following the predicted El nino rains, such settlements are at high risk of the effects of such which may include loss of life at a large scale. This will also assist in mobilization of adequate resources in time to prevent this from happening or in the worst case scenario, coordinate rescue efforts at a large scale.
